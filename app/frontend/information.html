About This Website

What is this place?

Welcome to Mood Reader! This website is designed as an interactive "laboratory" that bridges academic research with real-world applications. The primary purpose of this site is to serve as a "home" for the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) model I trained to recognize facial expressions (Facial Expression Recognition).

Specifically, the model you see here was developed using a transfer learning approach utilizing the EfficientNetB0 architecture. Rather than simply presenting the research in text form, this website allows you to interact directly with the results and see how deep learning technology interprets emotions.

This is an interesting study!

Facial Expression Recognition (FER) is a field of study that bridges the gap between human emotions and computational understanding. Technically, FER is a branch of computer vision and artificial intelligence (AI) that focuses on designing systems to automatically identify and classify human emotions—such as happiness, sadness, anger, or surprise—through the analysis of facial features in digital images or videos. In practice, this is often achieved by training deep learning models, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), on large-scale image datasets. The ultimate goal is to 'teach' machines to 'read' and understand fundamental human non-verbal cues, thus paving the way for more natural, empathetic, and responsive interactions between humans and technology.

Our Data Sources

Shoutout to these Datasets!

To train a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) model with a transfer learning approach using the EfficientNetB0 architecture, this study utilizes a comprehensive composite dataset.

This dataset is compiled from five benchmark facial expression recognition (FER) datasets that are widely recognized in the scientific community, namely:

  • JAFFE
  • FER-2013
  • RAF-DB
  • CK+
  • AffectNet

The model is specifically trained to recognize and classify seven universal facial expressions:

Angry, Disgust, Fear, Happy, Sad, Surprise, and Neutral.

Combining these five data sources results in a total of 80,096 facial expression images. The use of this large and diverse dataset aims to improve the model's generalization ability, ensuring it can learn from a wide variety of lighting conditions, backgrounds, and demographic variations to accurately recognize human expressions.
